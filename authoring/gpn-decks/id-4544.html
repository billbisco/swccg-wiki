 The Description

 The Strategy
 Back to Top 
It's not that hard to play.

Basically, try to get out twixes to activate massive amounts of force. Set up your hand through drawing and Threepio. With Goo Nee Tay out, the darkside won't do too much right away. Eventually, they'll set up characters somewhere, and you can just beat them down with mains. Resilience and Draw Their Fire make sure that your characters keep coming back for more.

Sense and Bith Shuffle combo are good for anti-evasion cards.

Clash and the Sorry About the Mess combos are good for character removal. Nothing hurts more than taking out one of their main guys before they even get to use them.

If you're lucky, your opponent won't be packing Evader & Monnok. Then feel free to play Revolution as you please. At my locals for example, people seem to have moved away from using decks that revolve around Crush the Rebellion. That allowed me to severely force choke them. In most games, my opponent will never activate more than ten force a turn.

The start:
Wokling (v): It gives you another force to activate and provides a late-game retrieval of one.

Colo Claw Fish: Since you go first, this prevents the opponent from using their own copy of this effect. It works wonders in a deck that has so many ones. The average destiny is fairly high, but this card makes it even better.

Your Insight Serves You Well: Makes hitting Jedi a piece of cake. You can't imagine how many times I swing at Vader and draw 5, 1. It also pulls Honor vs. walkers, Hunt Down, or Watto.

Card choices:
Corran x2: He's the man. He can set up beatdowns at non-battlegrounds (just don't get stuck there), and he can stop undercover spies like Arica. Plus he's a pilot for the Spiral.

Mechanical Failure: Who doesn't use Blizzard 4? Plus, it can setup a huge opportunity for a game-ending battle against walkers when combined with Corran.

Scrambled Transmission (v): Senate still works I hear. This should slow them down somewhat.

Enjoy.
- Chris Wodicka
