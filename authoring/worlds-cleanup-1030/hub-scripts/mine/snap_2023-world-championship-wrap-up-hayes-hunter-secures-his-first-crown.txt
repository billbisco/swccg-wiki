2023 World Championship Wrap-Up – Hayes Hunter Secures his first Crown
August 24, 2023 
The 28th annual Star Wars CCG World Championship took place the weekend of August 10-13 at the Hyatt Regency in Morristown, NJ, with five different sanctioned Players Committee Events spanning three days. The Main Event saw Hayes Hunter secure his first World Championship title, solidifying his well-deserved place among the other great SWCCG World Champions, defeating Casey Anis in the final confrontation.
Hayes Hunter, 2023 World Champion
The Main Event (“Day 1”) included an incredible group of 69 players and had eight games of Swiss play, leading into four play-in games to finalize the Top 8 cut. Bryan Mischke and Anthony Howard both finished Day 1 with a record of 7-1, both also seeking their first Worlds title. Ten total players finished 6-2; in order: Casey Anis, Hayes Hunter, Conor Britain, Joe Olson, Ryan Jellison, Bill Kafer, Brian Fred, Wayne Cullen, Scott Lingrell, and Jonny Chu. Conor, Scott, Wayne, & Bill lost their play-in games to Jonny, Joe, Ryan, & BFred, respectively. Joe, Hayes, and Ryan were the only holdovers from the Top 8 at 2022 Worlds, which was held in Atlanta. On Sunday (“Day 2”), Hayes defeated Joe and Ryan (who beat Bryan) in the quarterfinals and semifinals, respectively, while Casey defeated Jonny and BFred (who beat Anthony) en route to the final confrontation.
Hayes Hunter has been having a stellar 2023 so far, winning the US National Championship in Minnesota back in early April, which was his third Major title in the past few years. His domination at these two events this year has put him squarely in the lead for 2023 Player of the Year, so keep watching that leaderboard to see if anyone else can give him a run for his money with the remaining events this year.
Popular decks for the Main Event included a Tatooine: Desert Landing Site SSA(v), BHBM, ISB, & Walkers for Dark, and Communing (Obi-Wan & Yoda), Zero Hour, & Anakin Saga decks for Light. As usual, the Streaming Team, led by Dan Tartaglione, provided live commentary coverage of the event, with Brad Reinhold, Justin Miyashiro, Joe Olson, & Jeff Lavigne assisting with commentary duties throughout the weekend. All decklists are available on the PC website and also in GEMP-importable format, and all streams have been cut and posted to the PC’s YouTube Channel in their own playlist.
Main Event Top 8 – 
(L to R) Bryan Mischke, Jonny Chu, Brian Fred, Casey Anis, Ryan Jellison, Anthony Howard, Hayes Hunter, Joe Olson
On Friday evening, the traditional Team Tournament took place, with 16 teams duking it out for the coveted Glass Mug Trophies. Justin Desai and “Kashyyyk Train of Dominance” teammate Jonny Chu were victorious, with Scott Lingrell and Bill Bacheler finishing in second. Additionally on Friday, a throwback Retro event (Prem-DS2) was held with 8 participants, and saw Garrett Larson take first place with his HB Mains & ROps decks, and Joe Horbey coming in second place with TRM & ISB Scum.
Greg Shaw, Newly Inducted SWCCG PC Hall of Famer
During the pre-event announcements on Day 1 Saturday morning, long-standing volunteer, veteran player and pillar of the community Greg Shaw was formally inducted in the SWCCG PC Hall of Fame, joining the ranks of the other stand-out players and volunteers that have helped shape and represent our game and organization throughout the years. Greg’s award was presented by his good friend “Taco” Bill Kafer, and Greg’s parents were in attendance as well to watch.
Finally, the Consolation Event took place on Sunday while the Top 8 match-play for the Main Event played out in parallel. Cory Lauer finished in first place with a stellar 6-0 record in a field of 36 total players and won the main cash prize, with newly-inducted Hall of Famer Greg Shaw finishing in second place and also taking the deckbox prize for highest finish with a Jawa-format legal deck. 
The Main Event and all of its moving parts were run smoothly by Tournament Advocate Chris Schoenthal with rulings assistance from Rules Advocate, Greg Zinn. Dozens of player-volunteers made the weekend a success from every angle, and they cannot be thanked enough for their selfless contributions – contributions of the like that have been made for over 20 years now to keep our out-of-print card game going strong.
Numerous tournament reports have captured players’ experiences from the weekend, which included not only competitive and slightly-less-competitive Star Wars CCG, but of course also all the usual components of a wonderful atmosphere amongst friends amidst Morristown ventures, late night gaming and dinners out.
2023 World Championship – Group Photo
The Star Wars CCG community now turns its eyes towards the next in-person major events to wrap up the year:
–2023 Euro Championships (Bochum, Germany – Sept 8-10)
–2023 Endor Grand Prix (Seattle, WA – October 27-29)
–2023 Online Championship Series Playoffs (Oct-Nov – online, qualifying)
The Game will continue its momentum through the fall & winter, and on into the 2024 organized play schedule!
