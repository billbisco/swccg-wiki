2022 World Championship Wrap – Desai Wins Third Title
October 22, 2022 
2022 Worlds Main Event Streams
2022 Worlds Forum
Conor Britain
The 27th annual Star Wars CCG World Championship took place the weekend of October 14-16 at the Hyatt Regency Peachtree in Atlanta, Ga., with four sanctioned Players’ Committee Events spanning three days. The Main Event saw Justin Desai capture his third World Championship title (2013, 2015), defeating Matthew Harrison-Trainor in the final confrontation and joining Bastian Winkelhaus as the only three-time Star Wars CCG World Champion in the Game’s history.
Justin Desai – 2022 Star Wars CCG World Champion
Credit: Dan Tartaglione
The Main Event (“Day 1”) included 51 players and had two play-in games to finalize the Top 8 cut. Joe Olson finished as the lone 7-1 player, making his 7th Worlds Top 8, as he sought to win an unprecedented third consecutive Worlds title. Nine players finished 6-2; in order, Desai (record 13th Worlds Top 8 appearance), Ryan Jellison (1st Worlds Top 8), Greg Shaw (8th), Kyle Krueger (3rd), Hayes Hunter (5th), Harrison-Trainor (4th), Jeremy DiPaolo (1st), Chris Wirfs and Sam Tashima. Wirfs and Tashima lost their play-in games to DiPaolo and Harrison-Trainor, respectively. Olson and Shaw were the only holdovers from 2021 Worlds, which was held in Washington, D.C. On Sunday (“Day 2”), Desai defeated Krueger and Hunter in the quarterfinals and semifinals, respectively, while Harrison-Trainor defeated Shaw and Olson en route to the final confrontation.
2022 World Championship Top 8 Players: (L-R) Justin Desai, Matthew Harrison-Trainor, Greg Shaw, Jeremy DiPaolo, Joe Olson, Hayes Hunter, Kyle Krueger, Ryan Jellison.  Credit: Dan Tartaglione
Desai was clearly on a mission in 2022 to separate himself from the pack of four other two-time World Champions (Olson, Jonny Chu, Kevin Shannon and Emil Wallin) and join Winkelhaus in the three-timer club. After finishing ninth at both the 2021 US National Championship and 2021 World Championship, he finished in the Top 8 at both the 2022 Match Play Championship in January (dropped for Day 2 due to personal obligations) and the PC 20 Event in April. In the ramp-up to Worlds, Desai finished 67-5 in the Online Championship Series (across eight months) and was obviously determined to win his third Star Wars CCG obelisk trophy. Justin is slated to be the special guest on the October 26th episode of Star Wars CCG: Holotheatre (9:00pm Eastern).
Conor Britain
Popular decks for the Main Event included Shadow Collective and Carbon Chamber Testing for Dark, and overwhelmingly Yoda Communing followed by The Galaxy Needs A Legend for Light. As usual, the Streaming Team, led by Dan Tartaglione, provided live commentary coverage of the event, with Lenny Rubin and Casey Anis handling primary commentary duties. All decklists are available on the PC website and also in GEMP-importable format, and all streams have been cut and posted to the PC’s YouTube Channel in their own playlist.
Rewinding a bit on the weekend: on Friday, Throdo’s Cube Spectacular was held with a draft and a four-game event. In a 28-player field, Phil Aasen edged teammate Cal Aldred to win the event. Jonathan Murray led the organization and direction of the event, which will beget future cube events at in-person majors.
Event Within the Event – Teams Assigned for Main Event Performance with Placards on Home Area. Credit: Dan Tartaglione
On Friday evening, the traditional Team Tournament Event took place, with 12 teams duking it out for the coveted Glass Mug Trophies. Desai and “Kashyyyk Train of Dominance” teammate Steve Baroni were victorious, with Harrison-Trainor and Matt Lutz (“Drs. Matt”) finishing in second. Tartaglione was the tournament director for the Team Event.
Finally, the Consolation Event took place on Sunday while the Top 8 match-play for the Main Event played out in parallel. Jonny Chu – quite literally a stranger to Sunday Swiss (Consolation) Events since he has made every cut in every other major he has played in over the past twenty-two years – finished 5-1 and won the Enhanced Cloud City Dark Side Uncut Sheet. Ryan Sersen finished in second place. “Taco” Bill Kafer was the tournament director for the Consolation Event.
Kevin Jaap
The Main Event and all of its moving parts were run smoothly by Tournament Advocate Chris Schoenthal with rulings assistance from Rules Advocate, Greg Zinn. Dozens of player-volunteers made the weekend a success from every angle, and they cannot be thanked enough for their selfless contributions – contributions of the like that have been made for over 20 years now to keep our out-of-print card game going strong.
Numerous tournament reports and even a moving commemorative song have captured players’ experiences from the weekend, which included not only competitive and slightly-less-competitive Star Wars CCG, but of course also all the usual components of a wonderful atmosphere amongst friends amidst Atlanta ventures, late night gaming and dinners out.
The Star Wars CCG community now turns its eyes towards a couple online invitational events, one casual online event, and the next in-person major: 
–2022 Online Championship Series Playoffs (Oct-Dec – online, qualifying)
–GEMP AI Booster Bonanza  (Oct-Nov – online, casual)
–2022 Champions League (Nov-Dec – online, qualifying)
–2023 San Diego Open (San Diego, CA – Jan. 20-22). 
The Game will continue its momentum through the winter and into the 2023 organized play schedule!
