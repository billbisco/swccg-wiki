2021 World Championship Wrap – Olson Wins Again!
October 18, 2021 
2021 Worlds Main Event Streams
2021 Throwback Event Streams
2021 Worlds Forum
Banner by Ming Huo
The 26th annual Star Wars CCG World Championships took place the weekend of October 8th – 10th at the Hyatt Regency Crystal City in Arlington, Va., with four sanctioned Players’ Committee Events spanning three days. The Main Event saw Joe Olson not only defend his 2020 World Championship title in a final confrontation victory versus Matt Scott, but win an unprecedented fourth straight major. Also, he was inducted into the Star Wars CCG PC Hall of Fame.
Scott vs. Olson. Photo courtesy of Brandon Baity
The Main Event included 60 players, and had a clean cut of all 6-2 or better finishers making the Top 8. Olson finished 8th on “Day 1” (his 6th World Top 8 appearance), while Jared Napolitano finished first (2nd T8 app.), follow by fellow 7-1 performers Tom Haid (2016 World Champion; 3rd app.) and Scott. Greg Shaw (7th), Barry Alperstein, Robbie Hendon, Casey Anis (2nd) and Olson rounded out the Top 8; it was the first Top 8 Worlds appearance for Scott, Alperstein and Hendon while Napolitano and Olson were the only holdovers from 2020 Worlds, which was held online on GEMP. Olson defeated Shaw and Napolitano in the quarterfinals and semifinals, respectively, while Scott defeated Hendon and Alperstein en route to the final confrontation.
Top left clockwise: Napolitano, Olson, Haid, Alperstein, Shaw, Scott, Hendon, Anis. Photo courtesy of Brandon Baity
Joe Olson repeats as World Champ. 
Photo courtesy of Brandon Baity
Olson becomes just the 6th player to win two SWCCG World Championships, and only the second win the event in back-to-back years (Bastian Winkelhaus – 2018, 2019). Incidentally, Olson was inducted into the Hall of Fame by the Advocate Council and Tournament Committee, with his good friend Phil Aasen (2017 World Champion) announcing the accomplishment via a poem right before Game 1 of the Main Event kicked off. All five other players to win Worlds twice (or three times in the case of Winkelhaus) are in the Hall of Fame, as well (Jonny Chu, Kevin Shannon, Emil Wallin, Justin Desai and Winkelhaus).
Ming Huo
Popular decks for the Main Event included Carbon Chamber Testing, I Want That Map and Bring Him Before Me for Dark Side, and Master Qui-Gon Communing, Master Yoda Communing and Yavin 4 Operations for Light. As usual, the Streaming Team, led by Dan Tartaglione, provided live commentary coverage of the event, including the Throwback Event on Friday. “Hand cams” debuted, which provided more insights to player body language and hand contents than ever before.
On Friday, the 2021 Throwback Event was held, which was a 6-game Swiss Event using only cards printed through April 2001 (i.e., only Decipher cards and the “Premiere – Reflections II” meta). With a 15-player field, Steve Skilton went 6-0 and captured the win, while Napolitano, Matt Carulli and Kevin Elia finished 2nd through 4th, respectively. Watch Your Step was very popular and featured on the stream often. Decklists for the event were submitted and will be posted on the PC web page shortly.
Players get ready to kick off the Main Event. Photo courtesy of Bill Pittman
On Friday evening, the traditional Team Tournament Event took place, with 18 teams duking it out for the coveted Glass Mug Trophies. Team “Stop Batmouse Cold” – Dan Tartaglione and Brad Reinhold – won the event, with Team “Potential Allies” (Charlie Arlandson, Amar Banger) finishing in second place.
Finally, the Consolation Event took place on Sunday while the Top 8 match-play for the Main Event played out in parallel. Anthony Howard, known more endearingly as “Batmouse”, won the 28-player event, finishing 5-1. Ryan Jellison, Chris Gogolen and Sam Tashima finished 2nd, 3rd and 4th, respectively.
The Grand In-cake-ador. Photo courtesy of Brandon Baity
The tournament events were run smoothly by Tournament Advocate Chris Schoenthal with rulings assistance from Rules Advocate, Greg Zinn. In addition to Tartaglione, former Rules Advocate Tim Simon and Jan Westergard helped substantially with streaming efforts, and Bill Kafer, Hendon and Justin Miyashiro were also exceptionally active throughout the weekend in volunteer capacities. The event had a substantial amount of prizes in many different shapes and sizes. Lead Advocate Scott Lingrell, Prize Support Lead Chris Menzel and Marketing Advocate Napolitano organized the prize support with much help from the Marketing and Graphics Teams (the latter led by Ming Huo). The Grand Inquisitor theme manifested itself in several ways, including a sheet cake courtesy of Jeffrey Johns.
Late night gaming. Photo courtesy of Joe Olson.
Numerous tournament reports and some other forms of content have captured players’ experiences from the weekend, which included not only competitive Star Wars CCG but of course a “reunion”-type atmosphere amongst players in the community amidst local site seeing, late night gaming and plenty of group dinners.
The Star Wars CCG community now turns its eyes towards numerous more events on the docket:
–2021 OCS Playoffs (online, qualifying)
–2021 Jawa Cup (online, open)
-2021 Outrider Cup (online, invitational)
-2022 Match Play Championship (New Brunswick, N.J. – Jan. 21-23). 
The Game will continue its momentum through the winter and into the 2022 circuit!
